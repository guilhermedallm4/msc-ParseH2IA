% Information, Behavior, and Format: Where Syntax Lives in BERT Depends on Who Is
% Asking

\begin{abstract}
Where inside a fine-tuned Transformer does syntax form? We show that the answer
depends on the read-out used to ask the question. In a controlled study of
multi-task dependency parsers for Brazilian Portuguese (Porttinari), we cross
two decoder architectures (linear and biaffine heads) with two pretraining
regimes (monolingual BERTimbau, multilingual mBERT), and interrogate every
encoder layer with three read-outs: the deployed final-layer heads (logit
lens), per-layer trained linear probes (5 seeds, $\sigma \leq 0.003$), and two
causal ablations (early exit and block skip), with paired bootstrap tests over
1{,}683 test sentences. The three read-outs give three different answers.
Probes find part-of-speech information nearly saturated by layer~3 in all
encoders; the deployed heads only \emph{use} it from layers 6--9; and
attachment behavior is causally decided in the top quarter of the network---a
profile that replicates across independent training runs and transfers, at the
same \emph{relative} depth, from 12 to 24 layers. Most strikingly, single-token
probes recover head positions from the linear model's encoder (93.7\%) but
stay at chance-level (\!$\sim$26\%) on the biaffine encoder at every layer and
scale, although the biaffine parser attaches 90\% of tokens correctly: the
decoder dictates the representational \emph{format}, and format-blind probing
would declare a 90-UAS encoder syntax-free. Multilingual pretraining defers
syntactic behavior toward the top layers and its LAS penalty more than doubles
under the biaffine head ($+1.45$ vs.\ $+3.28$ points, $p<10^{-4}$). These
results caution that probing claims of the form ``layer $k$ encodes $P$'' are
not properties of an encoder, and that depth-truncation efficiency techniques
silently sacrifice exactly the structure that forms last.
\end{abstract}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:layerwise-discussion}

\paragraph{Information, behavior, and format are three different questions.}
Our central methodological finding is a three-way dissociation
(Table~\ref{tab:probes}, Figures~\ref{fig:per-layer} and~\ref{fig:probes}).
\emph{Information}: linear probes trained per layer find UPOS nearly saturated
by layer 3 ($\geq$98\% in all four encoders, including mBERT) and DEPREL by
layers 8--9---far earlier, and far more uniformly across models, than any
behavioral measure suggests. \emph{Behavior}: the deployed models, read
through their own final-layer heads, only commit to those decisions at layers
6--9 (UPOS) and 9--12 (DEPREL), and the early-exit ablation confirms the
behavioral profile causally. \emph{Format}: single-token probes recover head
positions from the linear model's encoder (93.7\% at layer 12) but remain flat
at 24--26\% on the biaffine encoders \emph{at every layer}, in the base and in
the 24-layer model alike---even though those parsers attach ~90\% of tokens
correctly. The explanation is architectural: a linear per-token head can only
solve attachment if the encoder compresses ``my head is at position $k$'' into
each token vector, whereas a biaffine scorer reads attachment from token
\emph{pairs}, so the encoder is never pressured to store it in a linearly
token-decodable form. A standard probing study, applied to the biaffine
encoder, would conclude that it does not represent syntactic heads at all.
Claims of the form ``layer $k$ of model $M$ encodes property $P$'' are
therefore not properties of an encoder: they are properties of an encoder, the
objective that fine-tuned it, and the read-out used to interrogate it.

\paragraph{The NLP pipeline is emergent; its depth is set by the decoder.}
All four models reproduce the ordering UPOS $\rightarrow$ DEPREL $\rightarrow$
HEAD along depth (Table~\ref{tab:convergence}, Figure~\ref{fig:task-depth}),
extending \citet{tenney2019bert} to a morphologically rich Romance language
and parsing-specific fine-tuning. The \emph{location} of each stage, however,
moves with the decoder: on BERTimbau, the biaffine head pulls behavioral UPOS
convergence from layer 8.3 to 6.1 and DEPREL from 9.2 to 8.8. Since the probes
show the underlying information profiles to be nearly identical across the two
encoders, this shift is a change in where the computation \emph{commits}, not
in where the information exists---reinforcing that behavioral and
informational depth must be reported separately.

\paragraph{The multilingual penalty is behavioral depth deferral.}
At the final layer, mBERT matches the monolingual encoder on tagging (98.9\%
vs.\ 99.2\% UPOS) but loses 1.0--2.9 UAS points depending on the decoder
(Table~\ref{tab:significance}). The per-layer curves locate the loss: mBERT
postpones syntactic resolution toward the top of the stack. With a linear
head, attachment at layer 11 is 64.6\% for mBERT against 76.3\% for BERTimbau,
and the final layer alone contributes $+28.1$ UAS points ($+17.4$ for
BERTimbau). The probes show a milder informational deferral (head
decodability at layer 8: 66.3\% vs.\ 80.4\%), so the behavioral bottleneck
amplifies an informational one. We interpret the deferral as the cost of
cross-lingual sharing: lower multilingual layers serve a language-neutral
lexical space, and language-specific syntactic configuration is assembled only
after the input is effectively situated in Portuguese. Crucially, the two
experimental axes interact: the monolingual LAS advantage is $+1.45$ points
$[+0.92, +1.98]$ under the linear head but $+3.28$ $[+2.56, +4.00]$ under the
biaffine head (both $p<10^{-4}$). A structured decoder relies on
representations that crystallize early enough to be refined; when the encoder
delivers them late, the decoder inherits the bottleneck. Architecture
rankings validated on monolingual encoders therefore need not transfer to
multilingual ones---which is where compute-constrained, low-resource
deployments actually use them.

\paragraph{The depth profile is robust: independent runs and $2\times$ scale.}
Two robustness checks argue that these profiles are properties of the training
setup rather than accidents of a single run. First, independent training runs
(ablation checkpoints trained without the UPOS task, i.e., under a different
multi-task mixture) reproduce final LAS within one point and yield
\emph{identical} half-resolution depths---the first layer reaching 50\% of
final UAS is 11/11 (linear/BERTimbau), 9/9 (biaffine/BERTimbau), and 11/11
(linear/mBERT) for main/independent runs (Table~\ref{tab:replication}).
Probe-level training variance is negligible (5 seeds, $\sigma \leq 0.003$).
Second, the profile transfers across scale at constant \emph{relative} depth:
in the 24-layer BERTimbau-large parsers, LAS remains below 10\% until
$\ell/L \approx 0.7$ and resolves in the top quarter of the network,
overlapping the 12-layer curves when depth is normalized
(Figure~\ref{fig:scale}). Depth deferral is measured in fractions of the
network, not in absolute layers---consistent with relative-depth analyses of
pretrained encoders and relevant to practitioners choosing truncation points
at different scales.

\paragraph{Depth is a compositional chain, not an ensemble of refiners.}
The block-skip ablation (Table~\ref{tab:block-skip}) rejects the reading of
Transformer depth as iterative refinement of a shared representation
\citep{jastrzebski2018residual}. Feeding embeddings directly into the last
block yields 27--54\% UPOS and $\leq$2.3 LAS across all five models (including
the 24-layer one); iterating that block to match the FLOPs of the full network
is \emph{worse than the raw embeddings} for tagging. The converse asymmetry
completes the picture: the bottom 8 layers alone retain 76--98\% UPOS but only
0.7--39.4 UAS. Lower layers build a manifold on which upper-layer functions
are defined; off that manifold, upper blocks are not merely suboptimal but
destructive. For efficient-inference research (early exit, LayerDrop, depth
pruning, distillation) the implication is task-dependent and sharp: tagging
tolerates truncation at layer 10 with $<$0.5 point loss, while relational
structure is destroyed ($-48.6$ UAS at layer 10 for the monolingual linear
model)---and multilingual encoders, the natural deployment target of such
techniques, are the most vulnerable because they concentrate syntax in
exactly the layers truncation removes.

\paragraph{Reading the saturation layer with calibrated heads.}
Retraining full head families (3 seeds, $\sigma \leq 0.003$) on the
\emph{frozen} 24-layer biaffine encoder at each task's saturation layer
(Table~\ref{tab:frozen}) closes the loop causally. With calibrated read-outs,
the saturation layers support near-deployed performance: UPOS at layer 10
costs 0.3 points, DEPREL at layer 18 costs 0.3, and attachment at layer 23
costs 0.4 UAS relative to the end-to-end model (91.10 vs.\ 91.52). And the
format finding survives calibration: a freshly trained pairwise (biaffine)
head recovers 89.4--91.2 UAS from layers 18--24, while a freshly trained
positional (linear) head extracts at most 34.4 UAS from the \emph{same}
frozen representations---even at the final layer (30.0), and decreasingly so
with depth (34.4 at 18 vs.\ 30.0 at 24): the encoder commits progressively to
the relational format as it refines. Sixty UAS points separate two trained
read-outs on identical inputs; that is the measurable distance between
information being present and being present in a token-decodable format.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Logit-lens numbers for intermediate layers remain lower bounds on decodable
information; the per-layer probes tighten this for token-level formats, and
the retrained biaffine heads act as calibrated pairwise probes for the
24-layer model---extending pairwise probing to every layer of every encoder
is future work. Our point is precisely that the choice of probe family
changes the conclusion.
Results cover one language and corpus (Brazilian Portuguese, Porttinari); the
scale replication uses the ablation checkpoint on the linear side (no
full-MTL linear-large checkpoint was retained), and fold-level retraining of
all four main configurations was not performed---the paired bootstrap
controls sentence-sampling variance, the independent-run replication and
probe seeds bound training variance partially. Decoding is greedy (no MST),
matching the deployed models under study.
